## Published version and author-code verification

The project's authoritative comparison is Li et al., *J. Phys. Chem. B* **130** (2026), 730–740, DOI [10.1021/acs.jpcb.5c08598](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcb.5c08598). The publisher supplement (`jp5c08598_si_001.pdf`, SHA-256 `2c565ab2aa60e94bc750852e60557413c525855dd11269cc9b8248a7e667f1ad`) was rechecked, pp. S3–S4. It confirms that PDBBind SEQRES sequences were separated by `:` between chains, RCSB header keywords defined functional categories, and cross-category protein similarity was set to zero. Same-category pairs were globally aligned and similarity defined by aligned matches divided by alignment length; the journal main text specifies matched residues. The supplement does not prescribe the handling of the colon delimiters, tie selection, or gap-scoring parameters.

The pinned author [similarity notebook](https://github.com/THGLab/LP-PDBBind/blob/cc363f80a6a696d562f290a87aa20173e60f6c52/dataset_creation/calc_similarities.ipynb), cells 3 and 25–27 and 30 (zero-based), was inspected on 2026-11-18. It uses `Bio.Align.PairwiseAligner`, BLOSUM62, gap-open −10, gap-extension −0.5, the first returned optimal alignment, and equal characters divided by alignment length. Cell 3 categorizes headers by sequential keyword assignments, so later matching keywords override earlier ones; `other` is the fallback. Cell 30 compares within one category but refers to `df` and `pdbids` without defining them in the saved notebook. Its aligner does not specify how `:` is handled, and BLOSUM62 does not contain `:`. It also uses the older `Bio.SubsMat` and parses a formatted alignment string, so software-version and rendering compatibility require verification rather than silent substitution.

The previously audited source CSV is accessible read-only at `../binding_affinity_v2/data/source/LP_PDBBind.csv`. Of the selected noncovalent cohort, 1,672/7,393 training, 268/958 validation and 383/2,171 test sequences contain `:`. The separator issue therefore affects a material part of the intended analysis. A direct check with the existing isolated environment's Biopython 1.88 and its BLOSUM62 matrix returned `ValueError: sequence contains letters not in the alphabet` for `AC:DE` versus `ACDE`; this demonstrates a current-environment failure, not the behavior of the authors' historic software. The published text and saved notebook do not establish the preprocessing used to align them; choosing removal, concatenation, or chain-wise comparison would be an assumption. We cannot honestly label a complete calculation exact until that issue and the original notebook inputs/behavior are reconciled. BDB2020+ sequence and category generation must also be verified independently. No model error analysis is performed yet.

Further source inspection: pinned `dataset_creation/prepare_info.ipynb` cell 13 uses `get_seq_` and collapses the chains to a single sequence **only if every chain sequence is identical**. Otherwise it joins all original chain sequences in their original order with `:`; it does not deduplicate mixed chains. Thus stripping colons preserves the recorded order but invents a continuous connection between chains. This is not established as the authors' method. The selected train/validation/test metadata have no empty sequences, missing `type`, or symbols outside BLOSUM62 other than `:`. The notebook's saved protein routine also handles `-` in its first sequence, but none appear in these selected sequences.

## Executable sensitivity audit

`scripts/protein_nearest_neighbor.py` implements the released category rule, notebook scoring and first returned optimal global alignment, with two explicit chain policies. `reject` excludes same-category sequence comparisons with `:` and reports `partial` where any candidate is excluded; such maxima are **lower bounds among the compared candidates**, not exact nearest-neighbor scores. `strip` removes `:` and treats the result as one continuous sequence; this is an exploratory approximation, not an established paper match. Cross-category comparisons score zero, and missing categories are excluded. Both policies report candidate and tie counts, failures, input and output hashes and Biopython version. Identical category/sequence queries and training sequences are cached. Tests cover the alignment and both policies. Biopython 1.88 and first-optimum behavior may differ from the historical environment.

A timing pilot with the frozen 7,393 training records and ten validation records took 29.4 seconds using the `strip` assumption on the existing machine; it was a timing check, not a representative cohort result. Full validation and test have 769 and 1,119 distinct category/stripped-sequence combinations respectively, so a full single-process run may take substantial time. No complete internal-cohort results are reported yet.

Once reconciled, the paper-matched candidate is the maximum similarity between each query and all 7,393 frozen training examples, where cross-category pairs receive the paper's zero and same-category pairs use its alignment definition. Report all ties, missing/invalid input, candidate counts and input provenance. Compare the cohort with the paper's Figure 1 cohort before interpreting agreement; these are not automatically the same population.

## What the paper specifies

The [journal article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13430955/) defines protein similarity by global Needleman–Wunsch alignment and the fraction of matched residues over the aligned sequence length. It reports maximum training-set similarity distributions for validation and test proteins. The article states a 0.5 training-to-evaluation threshold for its split, subject to its actual calculation conventions.

The publisher supplement confirms the corresponding description in the [arXiv v3 supporting information, sections S1.2–S1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.09639v3). The notebook supplies candidate gap scores and keyword precedence, but its saved source does not establish how it processed multichain sequence delimiters.

## Proposed independent nearest-neighbor calculation

This is a concrete all-category sequence audit designed for the frozen model, **not a claim of paper-exact reproduction**:

1. Restore the previously audited PDBbind protein PDB and ligand SDF files and the independent BDB2020+ files. Load frozen cohort IDs, verify identifiers and input hashes, and retain the 7,393 frozen training complexes as the comparison population. Coordinate preparations are not established to match the authors'.
2. For each complex, select every protein chain having a protein heavy atom within 5.0 Å of a ligand heavy atom. Extract each selected chain's ordered, coordinate-observed amino-acid sequence separately. Record chain IDs, lengths, unknown and modified residues, and missing or ambiguous inputs; do not replace failures with zero. Chain selection and residue conversion must be implemented and frozen explicitly before execution.
3. Globally align each query chain to each training chain using one pinned implementation of Needleman–Wunsch with BLOSUM62, gap open −10, gap extension −0.5 and penalized terminal gaps. Define and test an alignment tie rule before execution. These scoring choices are project defaults, not recovered author settings.
4. For each selected alignment calculate identity as the number of equal amino-acid residues divided by the total number of alignment columns including gap columns. Report aligned residue-pair coverage for each sequence separately; count unknown or nonstandard residues according to a fixed, documented rule established before running.
5. For a query/training complex pair, take the maximum identity across selected query-chain/training-chain pairs. For each query, take the maximum across all eligible training complexes. Record all ties or at minimum their count and a representative chosen deterministically by training complex ID and chain IDs.
6. Save query cohort and ID, nearest training ID, both chain IDs, identity, match and alignment-column counts, sequence lengths, both coverages, number of eligible training comparisons, tie count, failure/QC flags, input hashes, software version and parameters. Summarize excluded or failed complexes by cohort. Validate identical, different-length, missing-chain and tie cases and compare a small sample with an independent scalar calculation.

## Comparison and interpretation

The global alignment and maximum-to-training concept match the article at a high level. The proposed 5 Å contacting-chain selection, coordinate-observed sequences, separate-chain maximum, unrestricted comparisons and scoring defaults differ from or have not been verified against the authors' documented procedure. In particular, the preprint's concatenated `SEQRES` input and category-zero rule differ from this proposal. Thus numerical scores and nearest neighbors **cannot be called paper-matched**. An apparent threshold violation alone does not show an error in the paper; sequence source, categories, cohort, and alignment settings must first be reconciled.

For a paper-aligned audit, resolve colon handling and author input provenance, pin the compatible software and missing settings, and compare outputs on hand-checked pairs. Report the all-category independent audit separately, since assigning cross-category similarity zero can hide sequence relationships. BDB2020+ category and sequence provenance require separate verification. The similarity table can later be joined to frozen per-complex prediction errors; it does not itself establish an error–similarity relationship.
